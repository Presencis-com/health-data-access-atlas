# Global Health Data Access & Secondary-Use AI Gateway Atlas

Research-access routes describe ways to request data. Device-evidence and privacy-rule entries describe separate steps.
Flags describe the named route and its scope. Check the cited rules for the specific data and purpose.

## European Union · European Health Data Space (EHDS) health data access bodies

- Route type: research-access
- Legal basis: Regulation (EU) 2025/327, Articles 53(1)(e), 67(2), 68, 73 and 105.
- AI and secondary use: Qualifying scientific research in health or care can include training, testing and evaluation of algorithms, including AI systems and medical devices. Pseudonymised data requires justification when anonymous data will not suffice.
- Secure processing or data environment required: Yes
- Evidence: Applicant, professional roles and intended users · Permitted purpose, intended use and expected benefit · Requested data, sources, time range and formats · Justification for pseudonymised data, if requested · Description of any datasets brought into the environment · Safeguards, duration, tools and computing needs · Ethics assessment or opt-out exception justification where applicable
- Subgroup or bias evidence expected: No
- Disclosure controls expected: Yes
- Dates and timing: In force 26 March 2025. General application and specified secondary-use provisions from 26 March 2027. Chapter IV generally applies from 26 March 2029 and specified additional data categories from 26 March 2031. Article 68 allows three months to decide a complete permit application, extendable by three months with notice and reasons. Article 73 requires permits to use a secure processing environment with only non-personal data downloadable.
- [Official source 1](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/531b8c37-f962-11ef-b7db-01aa75ed71a1.0006.01/DOC_1)
- [Official source 2](https://health.ec.europa.eu/ehealth-digital-health-and-care/european-health-data-space-regulation-ehds_en)
- [Presencis regulation reference](https://presencis.com/regulations/ehds/)

## Finland · Findata

- Route type: research-access
- Legal basis: Act on the Secondary Use of Health and Social Data (552/2019).
- AI and secondary use: Individual-level permit data can support eligible scientific research, including qualifying AI studies. Development and innovation have a separate data request route for aggregate statistical data.
- Secure processing or data environment required: Yes
- Evidence: Applicable purpose and dataset definition · Current research plan and responsible organisation's research permit for scientific research · Ethics opinion where required · Processing location and users
- Subgroup or bias evidence expected: No
- Disclosure controls expected: Yes
- Dates and timing: Submit through Findata's e-service. Processing varies by complexity and whether data holders need to be consulted. Analyse individual-level data in Kapseli or another eligible secure environment. Findata checks outputs exported from Kapseli against anonymisation criteria.
- [Official source 1](https://www.finlex.fi/fi/lainsaadanto/2019/552)
- [Official source 2](https://findata.fi/en/permits/)
- [Official source 3](https://findata.fi/en/kapseli/)
- [Presencis regulation reference](https://presencis.com/regulations/)

## Germany · BfArM Research Data Centre for Health (FDZ Gesundheit)

- Route type: research-access
- Legal basis: Health Data Utilisation Act (GDNG) § 3 and Social Code Book V §§ 303d–303e.
- AI and secondary use: Section 303e includes development, training, validation and testing of AI systems in health care among permitted purposes. The applicant must justify why the requested data are needed for the purpose.
- Secure processing or data environment required: Yes
- Evidence: Applicant and authorised purpose · Why the data scope and structure are necessary · Justification for pseudonymised individual data where needed
- Subgroup or bias evidence expected: No
- Disclosure controls expected: Yes
- Dates and timing: Use the FDZ application portal for FDZ data. Individual datasets are processed under FDZ control. The access and coordination office established under GDNG § 3 is a separate function that BfArM describes as under development.

## France · Health Data Hub (Plateforme des données de santé) / SNDS

- Route type: research-access
- Legal basis: French health research authorisation and the national health data system (SNDS) access framework.
- AI and secondary use: Public-interest health research may request eligible SNDS data for a justified study, including AI research where it meets the applicable access conditions. The authorisation route uses CNIL review. Research strictly covered by an applicable reference methodology can follow its separate procedure.
- Secure processing or data environment required: Yes
- Evidence: Scientific protocol and public interest rationale · Requested data and linkage annex for SNDS where applicable · CNIL submission form and applicable privacy and security materials
- Subgroup or bias evidence expected: No
- Disclosure controls expected: Yes
- Dates and timing: For projects requiring authorisation, submit the dossier for review and CNIL decision. For data in the Health Data Hub warehouse, approved projects work on its technological platform.
- [Official source 1](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/demande-dautorisation-dune-recherche-en-sante-les-informations-fournir-et-les-criteres-doctroi)
- [Official source 2](https://www.health-data-hub.fr/starter-kit)
- [Official source 3](https://www.health-data-hub.fr/page/kit-documentaire-acces-base-de-donnees-catalogue-snds)
- [Presencis regulation reference](https://presencis.com/regulations/)

## United Kingdom · NHS England Secure Data Environment and Data Access Request Service

- Route type: research-access
- Legal basis: Secure data environment policy for NHS health and social care data in England and the NHS England DARS access process.
- AI and secondary use: Approved secondary uses, such as eligible research and analysis, can use the SDE under the Five Safes framework. AI work must fit the approved purpose and data terms.
- Secure processing or data environment required: Yes
- Evidence: Appropriate data access request with purpose and public benefit · Legal, ethical, security and information governance evidence · Approved users and data sharing terms
- Subgroup or bias evidence expected: No
- Disclosure controls expected: Yes
- Dates and timing: DARS timing depends on the application. Source data stay in the SDE under the policy, with tightly limited exceptions. Only outputs cleared for disclosure can leave the service.
- [Official source 1](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/secure-data-environment-policy-guidelines/secure-data-environment-for-nhs-health-and-social-care-data-policy-guidelines)
- [Official source 2](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/data-access-request-service-dars)
- [Official source 3](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/secure-data-environment-service)

## United Kingdom · Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA)

- Route type: device-evidence
- Legal basis: UK Medical Devices Regulations 2002, MHRA software guidance, UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018.
- AI and secondary use: Medical purpose and applicable device rules determine whether AI software is a regulated medical device. Device evidence does not grant data access. For use of personal health data, identify a UK GDPR lawful basis and special-category condition, with Data Protection Act safeguards where applicable.
- Secure processing or data environment required: No
- Evidence: Intended medical purpose and device classification · Technical and clinical evidence appropriate to device and market route · Documented data protection basis and safeguards where personal data are processed
- Subgroup or bias evidence expected: No
- Disclosure controls expected: No
- Dates and timing: MHRA published its change programme roadmap and updated it on 14 June 2023. Check the current device requirements for the intended market, including rules specific to Great Britain and Northern Ireland.

## United States · Food and Drug Administration (FDA)

- Route type: device-evidence
- Legal basis: FDA final guidance on predetermined change control plans for AI-enabled device software functions and demographic reporting in medical device clinical studies.
- AI and secondary use: Device submissions can include a predetermined change control plan for planned changes to an AI-enabled device software function. Clinical evidence should address relevant demographic representation and performance. Data access and privacy are handled separately.
- Secure processing or data environment required: No
- Evidence: Planned modifications, modification protocol and impact assessment for a proposed change control plan · Performance evidence matched to intended use · Evaluation and reporting by relevant age, race and ethnicity groups for applicable clinical studies
- Subgroup or bias evidence expected: Yes
- Disclosure controls expected: No
- Dates and timing: The cited final change-control-plan guidance is dated August 2025. Review timing depends on the applicable device submission pathway.
- [Official source 1](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/marketing-submission-recommendations-predetermined-change-control-plan-artificial-intelligence)
- [Official source 2](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/evaluation-and-reporting-age-race-and-ethnicity-specific-data-medical-device-clinical-studies)
- [Presencis regulation reference](https://presencis.com/regulations/)

## United States · HIPAA Privacy Rule de-identification

- Route type: privacy-rule
- Legal basis: 45 CFR § 164.514(a)–(c), including the Expert Determination and Safe Harbor methods in § 164.514(b).
- AI and secondary use: Information de-identified to the standard is outside HIPAA's protections for individually identifiable information. Access to identifiable data depends on a separate permissible route. This rule does not grant access to any dataset.
- Secure processing or data environment required: No
- Evidence: Expert Determination: qualified expert documents methods and results showing a very small identification risk · Safe Harbor: remove the specified identifiers and have no actual knowledge that the remainder could identify an individual
- Subgroup or bias evidence expected: No
- Disclosure controls expected: Yes
- Dates and timing: Apply and document the chosen de-identification method before treating data as de-identified. The rule does not set a permit decision clock.
- [Official source 1](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title45-vol2/xml/CFR-2025-title45-vol2-sec164-514.xml)
- [Presencis regulation reference](https://presencis.com/regulations/)

## Canada · Health Canada and provincial health-data routes

- Route type: device-evidence
- Legal basis: Health Canada pre-market guidance for machine learning-enabled medical devices and, as a provincial example, Ontario's Personal Health Information Protection Act, 2004 (PHIPA) § 44.
- AI and secondary use: The federal guidance covers Class II–IV device licence applications and relevant subgroup testing. Data access is a separate step. Ontario PHIPA § 44 describes one route for a custodian to disclose personal health information for research under specified safeguards.
- Secure processing or data environment required: No
- Evidence: Device intended use, training and evaluation data description and class-appropriate performance and clinical evidence · Results for relevant subgroups and uncertainty where applicable · For Ontario § 44 disclosure: written research plan, research ethics board decision and custodian agreement
- Subgroup or bias evidence expected: Yes
- Disclosure controls expected: Yes
- Dates and timing: The cited pre-market guidance is dated 1 April 2026. Timing for provincial data access depends on the custodian and province.
- [Official source 1](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/pre-market-guidance-machine-learning-enabled-medical-devices.html)
- [Official source 2](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03)
- [Presencis regulation reference](https://presencis.com/regulations/)

## Australia · Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA)

- Route type: device-evidence
- Legal basis: Therapeutic Goods Act 1989 and Therapeutic Goods (Medical Devices) Regulations 2002, including software-specific classification rules.
- AI and secondary use: Software with an intended medical purpose can be regulated as a device, including software using AI. The regulatory route turns on the intended purpose and applicable classification, exclusions or exemptions. Access to health data for training is a separate matter.
- Secure processing or data environment required: No
- Evidence: Intended purpose and applicable software classification · Evidence of conformity with applicable essential principles and risk-class requirements
- Subgroup or bias evidence expected: No
- Disclosure controls expected: No
- Dates and timing: Check the applicable conformity assessment and inclusion process before supplying regulated software. Timing varies by classification and route.
- [Official source 1](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03952/latest/text)
- [Official source 2](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2002B00237/latest/text)
- [Presencis regulation reference](https://presencis.com/regulations/)

## Hong Kong · Hospital Authority research data access and Data Collaboration Lab (HADCL)

- Route type: research-access
- Legal basis: Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (Cap. 486) and the Hospital Authority mechanism for external academic research data requests.
- AI and secondary use: The Hospital Authority assesses external academic research requests case by case. The research purpose and data needs must support the request. Appropriate off-site provision is possible under the separate research request mechanism.
- Secure processing or data environment required: No
- Evidence: Research purpose and requestor details · Data fields, population and format needed for the study · Privacy protections and supplementary information requested during assessment
- Subgroup or bias evidence expected: No
- Disclosure controls expected: Yes
- Dates and timing: The legacy Lab page reports a transition to new services. The separate external request process describes expedited and customised applications, with longer turnaround for complex requests.
